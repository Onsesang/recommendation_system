# Experiment 22 — Open-source tactile predictor benchmark

Generated by `scripts/07_report.py` from `results/*.csv`. All numbers are read
from those files; none are typed in by hand.

---

## 1. Research question

> *"If a current general-purpose VLM can already judge fabric feel from a garment
> photograph, why build review-derived tactile supervision and fine-tune
> FashionCLIP at all?"*

Our predictors are compared against three kinds of open baseline:

- **A. generic open VLM** — Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, scored by constrained YES/NO logits
- **B. texture / material specialist** — the CLIP-Texture protocol, SigLIP2, FashionCLIP zero-shot
- **C. image → tactile-property specialist** — see §3, none turned out to be executable

Primary metrics are **macro AUROC** and **macro AUPRC**, threshold-free, because
experiment 21 showed that macro F1 on this data tracks the positive base rate
rather than discrimination. F1 is reported as secondary only.

### The tactile-specialist track has no executable member

**`Teaching Cameras to Feel` (Purri & Dana, ECCV 2020) is NOT DIRECTLY EXECUTABLE.**
The official repository `github.com/matthewpurri/Teaching-Cameras-to-Feel` contains
only `LICENSE`, `README.md`, `index.md`, `assets/` and `imgs/` — **no source code,
no inference script and no released weights**, just a Google Drive link to the SPS
dataset. Reimplementing its cross-modal adversarial model and retraining on SPS
would far exceed the 2-hour environment-recovery budget, and its output space
(15 continuous BioTac instrument readings: friction, compliance, adhesion,
texture, thermal conductance) has no EXACT mapping to our semantic taxonomy.

The other candidates found by search fail the RGB-only condition and are recorded
as related work rather than baselines: FabricVST / Multimodal ZSL (Cao et al. 2024)
requires a tactile sensor reading at inference **and** is trained on the very
fabrics we hold out; TactStyle (CHI 2025) outputs fabrication heightfields, not
tactile attributes; Li (2025) requires touch input.

> **So the honest answer to "is there an open image→tactile specialist to compare
> against?" is: not one that can be run.** That absence is itself a finding, and it
> is the reason the specialist column of this benchmark is empty rather than
> filled with a number we manufactured.

### Substitution recorded

The CLIP-Texture protocol paper (`cvl-umass/clip-texture`, ECCV CVinW 2022)
releases **no checkpoint of its own** — its contribution is an evaluation showing
stock CLIP is a strong texture baseline. We therefore reproduce its protocol with
the encoder it reports. We use **ViT-L/14** rather than **ViT-L/14@336** because the
336 repository ships only a pickled `.bin`, which `transformers` refuses to load on
`torch<2.6` (CVE-2025-32434); the paper's own tables put the two within 0.3 pp on DTD.

## 4. Taxonomy mapping

Frozen in `artifacts/taxonomy_mapping.json` / `.md` **before any model was scored**,
and inherited verbatim from `experiments/21_fabricvst_external/config.json` rather
than re-derived here.

- **EXACT: 8** — `soft`, `rough`, `smooth`, `thick`, `thin`, `cool`, `warm`, `stiff` — the only attributes in the main comparison
- **APPROXIMATE: 2** — `stretchable↔elastic`, `fluffy↔spongy` — supplementary only
- **UNAVAILABLE: 18**

Deliberate non-mappings carried over: `firm` is **not** mapped to FabricVST `stiff`
(in last2, `firm` is the antonym of `soft`, a different axis from `stiff`/`flexible`);
`flexible` is **not** mapped to `stretchable` (bending compliance ≠ extensibility).

One caveat is flagged rather than hidden: `stiff` is recorded EXACT on surface form
and gloss, but last2 treats `stiff` as the antonym of `flexible` whereas FabricVST
lists it opposite `soft`.

## 5. Evaluation protocol

**Scoring.**
- *Our heads* (Last2, FabricVST-A/B, frozen FashionCLIP head): independent sigmoids.
- *CLIP family*: paired positive/negative text prompts, score =
  `softmax([s·cos(f(x), t_pos), s·cos(f(x), t_neg)])[0]` with the model's own logit
  scale. Three prompt templates were written in advance; the one used on test is
  chosen by **macro AUROC on the Amazon development split** (`artifacts/clip_prompt_selection.json`).
- *VLMs*: one fixed yes/no question per attribute, prompt version `v1.0-20260917`.
  `P(YES)` is read from the **first answer token's logits** as
  `softmax over {max logit of YES-variants, max logit of NO-variants}`. This is a
  single forward pass, so it is deterministic by construction (equivalent to
  temperature 0) and is a genuine model probability — **not** a self-reported
  `"confidence": 0.8`.

**Thresholds.** Every model's per-attribute threshold is chosen by F1 on the Amazon
**development** split over a 0.05–0.95 grid, then transferred unchanged to Amazon
test and to FabricVST. FabricVST has no validation set of its own, so this is the
"source-domain validation threshold" option; external primary metrics are AUROC and
AUPRC, which need no threshold. **No threshold was ever tuned on a test result.**

**Uncertainty.** Macro AUROC 95 % CIs come from a 2,000-replicate bootstrap
resampling *evaluation units* — products on Track A, fabrics on Track B.

### What these tables say

**In our own target distribution, review supervision pays for itself.**
Last2 (ours) reaches macro AUROC **0.7345**, against
**0.5589** for the category-only control that never sees a pixel:
paired bootstrap over evaluation units puts Last2 **+0.1760** macro AUROC ahead of the category-only control, 95 % CI [+0.1266, +0.2246] — **the interval excludes zero**.
So the question in §1 — "why not just use an open model?" — has a concrete answer
on Track A: the open models tested do not reach our accuracy on apparel photographs.

**Out of distribution the advantage disappears.** On FabricVST the leading model is
**SigLIP2-so400m** at **0.6755**, against Last2's
**0.5752**. Two things must be said precisely here, and the second is easy to
overstate:

1. **Our predictors do not generalise.** Last2's macro AUROC CI on the 50 fabrics is
   [0.4707, 0.6756], which **includes 0.5**. We cannot
   distinguish it from chance on external fabric swatches. That is the solid claim.
2. **Whether the generic encoder actually *beats* us there is a weaker claim.**
   paired bootstrap puts the gap at **+0.1002** macro AUROC, 95 % CI [-0.0081, +0.2048] — **the interval contains zero, so the ordering between SigLIP2 and Last2 is not established**.
   With only 50 evaluation units, a higher point estimate is not by itself evidence
   of a real ordering, and we do not assert one beyond what the interval supports.

Last2's Track B macro AUROC reproduces experiment 21's independent measurement
(0.5843 over all 225 crops) to within the crop-subsampling difference, which is a
consistency check on this pipeline rather than a new result.

The mechanism experiment 21 identified is reproduced here as a general diagnostic:
for an all-positive predictor, F1 = 2p/(1+p) is a pure function of the base rate.
`plots/f1_vs_base_rate.png` plots observed F1 against that quantity — any model
sitting on the diagonal is reporting the base rate, not skill.
`plots/prediction_rate_vs_prevalence.png` shows the same thing per attribute.

This is why every headline in this report is AUROC and AUPRC.

## 10. External generalization

Covered in §6 Track B. The short version: on 50 held-out fabrics our
review-supervised predictors sit near chance, and the strongest model is a generic
open encoder we did not train. Combined with experiment 21's diagnosis
(pseudo-label positive bias from reviewers asserting presence but not absence),
the most defensible reading is that the Amazon-trained tactile signal is
domain-specific rather than a general fabric-feel predictor.

## 11. Limitations

1. **Pseudo-labels, not gold.** Every Track A target is a Qwen-extracted,
   review-derived label. No human gold set exists for this project
   (experiment 15: 5 of 160 completed). Nothing here measures real tactile accuracy.
2. **Shared GPU.** The A100 used here was occupied by another user's LongBench job
   for the first hours of this experiment. We stayed off the device rather than
   contend for it — after our 7 GiB allocation contributed to an OOM that killed one
   of their processes. Precisions actually used are recorded in
   `logs/vlm_precision_used.json`; any model not run in BF16 is flagged there.
3. **Crop subsampling.** Track B uses 24 of 225 crops per fabric so
   that a VLM can be afforded. The subset is identical across models, but it is a
   subset.
4. **Prompt sensitivity.** CLIP-family results depend on the prompt template; we
   mitigate this by selecting among three fixed templates on development and
   reporting all three in `artifacts/clip_prompt_selection.json`.
5. **The specialist baseline is missing**, not because we skipped it, but because no
   runnable open RGB-only tactile-property model with a mappable output exists (§3).
6. **`stiff` axis mismatch** between the two taxonomies (§4).

---

## 12. Conclusion

1. On apparel photographs from our own distribution, **review-supervised
   fine-tuning leads every open zero-shot alternative we could run and beats the
   no-pixel category control**, with a paired interval excluding zero. That is the
   justification for the pipeline.
2. **On external fabric swatches it does not generalise** — Last2's interval there
   includes chance. A generic open encoder has the higher point estimate, but at 50
   fabrics we do not claim a established ordering between the two.
3. **There is no executable open image→tactile-property specialist to compare
   against.** The nearest published model ships no code or weights.
4. Macro F1 remains unusable as a headline on this data; the base-rate diagnostic
   in §8 shows why.
